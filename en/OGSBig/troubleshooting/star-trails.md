# Star point pull line, blur or abnormal tracking direction

Star point problems usually arise from tracking orientation, polar axis, mechanical stability, camera vibration, or exposure parameters. It is recommended to only change one condition at a time.

# # Quick judgment

1. Check whether the hemisphere selected on the control interface is correct. If the tracking direction is opposite, the star point will quickly pull the line.
2. Reduce the focus length and exposure time to half of the original values for testing.
3. Make sure the tripod, equatorial mount, ball head, camera and lens are all locked.
4. Re-polar alignment; for telephoto, use polar scope or drift method first.
5. Check whether the center of gravity of the load is too high, whether it is obviously tilted to one side, and whether the cable is pulling on the camera.
6. Use shutter pre-delay, electronic front curtain or remote shutter to reduce button and reflector vibration.

# # Check based on star point performance

| Performance | Priority Check |
| --- | --- |
| Each picture is obviously elongated in the same direction | Hemisphere direction, whether to start tracking, polar axis deviation |
| Some photos are clear, some are shaken | Wind, ground vibration, tripod, shutter vibration, cable |
| Sharper center of frame, radial edges | Lens aberration, aperture and focus, not necessarily tracking issues |
| The longer the exposure, the more obvious the wires are | Polar accuracy, periodic error, focal length and pixel scale |
| The equipment has jamming or abnormal sounds | Stop immediately and cut off the power, and check the mechanical interference and load |

# # Suggestions when submitting a question

- Lens focal length, camera model and exposure time.
- Total load weight and installation photos.
- Hemisphere and polar alignment method.
- An original star point photo and a 100% enlarged screenshot.
- Wind conditions, ground and tripod conditions at the time.
