# Drift method polar alignment description (highest accuracy method)

The drift method is the **most accurate polar-alignment method** and is suitable for high-precision imaging and telephoto systems.

The method:

No need for a North Star
Applicable to any region around the world.

---

# Applicable scenarios

Recommended:

* Telephoto photography (≥300mm)
* Deep space photography
* Unable to see Polaris
* Southern Hemisphere
* High-precision demand scenarios

Accuracy:

**Highest**

But it takes longer.

---

# Introduction to principles

When the polar axes are not perfectly aligned:

The star will be in the field of view:

**Slow drift**

By observing the drift direction and adjusting:

Ultimately it can be achieved:

**Precise polar alignment**

---

# Basic operation process

# # 1. Select a reference star

Select:

Stars close to the celestial equator.

Requirements:

Moderate brightness
easy to observe

---

# # 2. Observe the drift direction

Use:

Long exposure or live view

Observe the stars:

Whether north-south drift occurs.

---

# # 3. Adjust the azimuth angle

If drift occurs:

Adjustments:

**Left and right directions (azimuth angle)**

until the drift decreases.

---

# # 4. Select another star

Select:

Stars near the eastern or western horizon.

Observations:

Drift up and down.

---

# # 5. Adjust latitude angle

by:

**latitudeAdjustment knob**

Adjustments:

until the drift disappears.

---

# # 6. Repeat fine-tuning

Repeat the above steps:

until:

The star drifts almost without drift.

Complete:

High-precision polar alignment.

---

# Usage suggestions

Recommended:

* Deep space photography
* Telephoto system
* Fixed long-term observation equipment

Note:

This method takes a long time, but is extremely accurate and is a professional-grade alignment method.
